# E4-D2 · Frozen representation audit

96 dedicated normal-start, uniform-play GameCore source games, 64 training and 32 test games. Every ply congruent to 3 modulo 17 supplies a pre-action public observation. Reconstruct the full public history and project every legal MOVE using Chase v4, rejecting banned replies. All threat labels use the opponent's immediately legal responses after that candidate. No positions from this diagnostic are used to train any policy. This uniform-play population differs from the small certified endgames in D1b. Do not infer a causal connection between the two tasks from a difference in group means.

Local-only requires all recaptures of the moved piece to be one orthogonal step. Far-only requires all recaptures to be cannon captures at distance three or greater. The opened-ray subset requires the original source to lie strictly between a recapturing cannon and the candidate target: vacating one blocker opens the one-screen shot. Distance-two and mixed-exposure examples are excluded from these comparisons. Negative examples have no immediate recapture of the moved piece; this limited safety label is not a game-theoretic action value. Immediate winning candidates are excluded.

The five trained B policies are frozen. The only learned diagnostic weights are separate balanced ridge readouts (train-only feature standardization and fixed penalty). For every test state and separately for capture/quiet MOVE, compare all eligible safe and exposed action pairs. Give ties half credit, average first within the state/action-type group, then across groups and training seeds. Each probe scores exposure; the actual policy is scored in the opposite direction (higher logit for safe). Window counts, positives, eligible source games, seed-level readouts and descriptive intervals are in results.json and contrasts.json. The common-group far vs local policy comparison uses identical source game, ply and capture/quiet class. The bootstrap resamples source games and model training seeds independently while keeping method pairs together. Intervals condition on the fixed probe training games.

Probe inputs in order: seven action-geometry channels; then spatial embeddings at both endpoints; then global context; then existing frozen CNN cell vectors along all four rays from the target, ordered by distance and zero padded off-board. The last probe changes the readout inputs and increases its dimension. It has no access to the action's successor state or threat labels at test time. The result quantifies accessibility to this readout; it neither isolates the effect of dimension nor demonstrates the playing strength of a new actor. An additive linear context term is constant across actions in a given state; the real MOVE scorer can have nonlinear context interactions.

Exploratory proof readout in win-probe.py: train on D1 and test on disjoint D1b deck IDs, use only certified win and certified nonwin actions, and omit every Unknown. State-only, full scorer inputs, trained scorer hidden features and the frozen policy log probability are compared. Pooled AUC is sensitive to between-state and proof-selection differences. Within-root action discrimination requires examples of BOTH verified labels in the same root. Inadequate root coverage is reported as missing evidence.
